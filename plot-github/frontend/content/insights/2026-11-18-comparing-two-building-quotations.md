# Two builders, two prices. What is each one pricing?

One quotation includes roof sheets and installation. Another says “roofing works” and leaves the covering to the owner. Both can look complete on the first page. Compare one trade at a time before looking at the totals.

## Put both quotes beside the same drawing

Check the drawing revision and date on each offer. If one builder priced an earlier layout, send the current drawings to both and ask them to mark what has changed. Even a small change in the number of bathrooms can affect several trades.

## Follow one item from supply to finish

Take the floor tiles. For each quotation, write down the area, the tile allowance, who buys the tiles, who pays delivery, and whether adhesive, grout, skirting and laying are included. A blank line is a question to ask. Record the answer beside the quoted figure so you can refer back to it later.

## Keep provisional amounts visible

If you have yet to select kitchen cabinets, ask what the allowance covers. Does it assume a particular length, finish or worktop? Keep that amount visible beside the confirmed work. You can then revisit the same line when you choose the kitchen, instead of trying to reconstruct the whole price.
