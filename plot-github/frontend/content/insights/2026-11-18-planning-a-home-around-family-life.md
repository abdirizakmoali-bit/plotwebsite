# Walk through your floor plan before you build

Imagine arriving home with shopping while guests are sitting in the lounge. Trace your route from the gate to the kitchen. That two-minute exercise can reveal more about a layout than the bedroom count.

## Draw the routes you use every day

Print the plan and give each route a different colour. Trace a visitor going from the entrance to the guest bathroom, a child moving from a bedroom to the family living space, and someone carrying laundry outside. Mark any point where a route crosses a space you want to keep private.

## Test the room with your own furniture

Measure the sofa, dining table or wardrobe you expect to keep. Ask your designer to place it on the plan at the correct scale. Then look at the doors. Can someone enter the room while a wardrobe is open? Can the family pull out dining chairs and still walk through to the kitchen?

## Take three decisions to the design meeting

Choose the three places where the plan works least well for your household. Describe the difficulty in everyday terms, such as “visitors pass the bedroom doors to reach the bathroom”. Give the designer room to suggest a solution. Keep a dated copy of the revised plan and walk the same routes again.
