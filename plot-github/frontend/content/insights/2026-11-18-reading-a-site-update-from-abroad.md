# How to read a site update from abroad

A photograph can tell you that blocks arrived on Monday. It takes a little more information to know whether the walls you paid for are up. Here is a weekly reporting routine for anyone following a build from outside Hargeisa.

## Ask for the same view each week

Agree a few camera positions with the site team. A wide shot from the gate gives context. A second view from the same corner of the house makes changes easier to spot. Ask for the date, room or grid reference and a short caption on each photograph. Close-ups are most useful alongside a wider view that shows where they were taken.

## Match the update to the programme

Suppose the team planned to lay the external walls this week. The report might say that the north and east walls are complete, while the west wall is waiting for a window detail. That gives you a precise question for your next call. Keep material deliveries, work installed and work checked on separate lines.

## End the call with a decision list

For each item needing your approval, write down the choice, who will confirm its cost or programme effect, and when the team needs an answer. Send the agreed list back after the call. Next week, open the same list before looking at the new photographs.
